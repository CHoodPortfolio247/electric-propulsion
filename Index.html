How Does Electric Propulsion Work?

Electric thrusters use electrical energy to accelerate propellant to high exhaust velocities. They are used on satellites and spacecraft.

Why Do We Use It?

Electric thrusters can achieve large changes in spacecraft velocity while using less propellant than chemical rockets. However, they generally produce much less thrust.

Atmospheric Applications

Electroaerodynamic propulsion uses high-voltage electrodes to ionize nearby air. An electric field accelerates these ions, which collide with neutral molecules and create airflow called ionic wind.

Engineering challenges include thrust density, electrical power requirements, drag, and high-voltage breakdown.

Where Is It Going?

Researchers continue developing electric propulsion for satellites and deep-space missions. Atmospheric research also explores applications in propulsion and airflow control.
